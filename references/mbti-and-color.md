# MBTI and color associations

## MBTI

Start with the user's reported type or assessment result and the interaction they want to understand. Describe any type-based suggestion as a preference to check against experience. If no type is supplied, ask about one relevant preference or discuss the behavior directly. Treat informal quizzes as self-reflection. Avoid assigning an official type from a brief conversation or inferring affection, ability, compatibility, or a clinical condition from a type code.

For example, someone who prefers to think before speaking might benefit from time to collect their thoughts. Ask how they actually respond in conflict and whether agreeing on a time to resume would help both people. Their own answer guides the advice. [Myers & Briggs Foundation guidance on ethical use](https://www.myersbriggs.org/unique-features-of-myers-briggs/ethical-use-of-the-mbti/)

## Color psychology

Ask what the color is used for and what the person actually feels around it. Consider the setting, cultural associations, personal memories, and visual conditions such as lighting or saturation when relevant. Research finds shared patterns alongside geographic and linguistic variation. A color preference provides a starting point for discussion. Avoid using it to infer personality or mental health or promising that a color will change mood. When a choice is practical, suggest comparing options under similar conditions. [Jonauskaite et al., 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32900287/)

## Combined example

User: “I identify as INFJ, dark blue makes me feel calmer, and I drew Temperance. My partner wants to settle disagreements immediately.”

Reading: “I read Temperance here as a prompt about timing. Your type gives us a reason to ask whether you prefer time to think. Your experience with dark blue suggests a setting you might choose for that pause. The useful step is to agree on when the pause ends. Try saying, ‘I want to finish talking about this. Can we take twenty minutes and come back at eight?’” If the user asks for a closing line, give an original one in their chosen language.
